If you've ever wondered what it's like to cross a border for the first time in your 30s, know that the feeling is a mix of airport anxiety and pure awe. This was my very first international trip. Buenos Aires was the destination. And, honestly? I couldn't have picked a better place.

Well... let's get straight to the point.

## The Beginning and the Flight

I prepared a rigorous checklist ([TDAH](/en/notes/glossary#TDAH) and [TAG](/en/notes/glossary#TAG) demand it). I chose to check in the large [Cavezalle](/en/notes/glossary#Cavezalle) suitcase — the peace of mind was worth the extra fee. And I knew I was going to need space to bring back the things Laura had recommended.

The Boeing 737 experience was loud. [ANC](/en/notes/glossary#ANC) saved my brain during the flight from Curitiba (CWB) to [Aeroparque](/en/notes/glossary#Aeroparque) (AEP).

Landing at Aeroparque was smooth, and the tip to catch an Uber or Cabify by logging into the airport's Wi-Fi (and ignoring the autonomous taxis) worked perfectly. Golden tip: have a Wise or Nomad card enabled on Apple Pay to get the [MEP](/en/notes/glossary#MEP) exchange rate.

## What I Found in the City

Buenos Aires is intense. The July cold (6°C to 16°C) requires a layering strategy, but the city warms you up with culture.

### San Telmo and the Antique Shops

[San Telmo](/en/notes/glossary#San-Telmo) is the neighborhood I would live in Buenos Aires. Antiques in every shop. I saw everything from an old typewriter to a horned skull decorating a vintage office desk.

There's an artisan market where I watched a guy making scrap metal robots by hand. [DIY](/en/notes/glossary#DIY) in its veins — do it yourself, no permission needed.

### La Boca and the Devotion to Maradona

[La Boca](/en/notes/glossary#La-Boca) is an open-air museum. I went in with zero expectations, but I left with the image of Diego Maradona burned into my retinas. The guy is a religion here — a mural on every corner, massive and colorful graffiti. I walked to [La Bombonera](/en/notes/glossary#La-Bombonera) and took a selfie with the statue of my boy [Riquelme](/en/notes/glossary#Riquelme) (accepting the risk of losing my phone, but the experience was worth it).

### Parque Centenario and the Museum

In [Parque Centenario](/en/notes/glossary#Parque-Centenario), the architecture and sculptures surprised me. I found a bas-relief sculpture inside a concrete box — very beautiful. But the real saga was trying to find the amphitheater, ending up in front of a museum, and using up all my Spanish to find out the entrance fee was 67,000 pesos for "brasileños". I didn't go in, but the walk was worth it.

I also bought a Che Guevara book and an English dictionary at a street market. I had a chat with a vendor who had tried learning Portuguese from an ex-girlfriend. Speaking Spanish was hard, understanding it too, but that was when I realized the trip was really happening.

## Final Thoughts

My first time outside Brazil had cold weather, airport fear, culture shock, but a lot of beauty. Buenos Aires doesn't hide what it is. It flaunts its gothic and neoclassical architecture while simultaneously pulling you into the reality of the streets in La Boca.

I still have a lot of photo galleries to organize (I took over 1200 photos and 87 videos), but I can safely say: you never forget the first stamp on your passport (or showing your ID, in the case of Mercosur).
